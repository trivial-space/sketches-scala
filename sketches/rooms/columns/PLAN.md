# Plan: `rooms/columns` — colonnade courtyard on the room stage, with pillar paintings

## Context

An 84 × 168 m courtyard: 40 m columns on a 14 m lattice (two rings: one engaged
in the perimeter walls, one free-standing a bay further in) and a full grid of
balks across the top. Today it is drawn with a debug shade (normals / raw UV).

Two stages:

1. **Stage 1 — onto the room stage.** Rebuild it on `sketchlib.utils.room` and
   the room templates' material: noisy ambience bakes, a luminous plane above
   the balk grid with irregular bloom, mirror floor, then a tile pattern on the
   floor.
2. **Stage 2 — wall paintings.** Every wall bay between two columns gets a
   generated pillar painting (the `paintings/pillar` algorithm), painted
   **directly into the wall texture** — no hung canvases, no `Hanging`, no drop
   shadows.

**The geometry is preserved** — the lattice, the two column rings, the balk
grid. Only how it is _built_ (ring data instead of hand-placed instances) and
how it is _textured_ change. This stays a regular sketch: lean code, section
markers, tunables in one block at the head; the why lives here.

### The geometry constants are live

`ColSpace`, `ColHeight`, `ColWidth`, `BalkHeight`, `ColsXHalf`, `ColsZHalf` are
hand-tweaked at any time to change the look. **Nothing may treat their current
values as final**:

- Everything structural is **derived** from them in code — ring points, bay
  widths, `SoffitY`, the balk clip rect, light overhang, far plane, texture
  sizes, counts. No literal coordinate or count anywhere.
- Only **proportions of the architecture** are relative to them: pillar width as
  a fraction of the bay's clear width, pillar height as a fraction of `SoffitY`,
  `CofferDepth` relative to `BalkHeight`.
- **Material detail is absolute, in meters**, and tuned independently of the
  geometry: noise feature sizes, grime, edge fades, slab size, paint grain,
  `PillarFloorGap`. Making the courtyard bigger does not make its plaster
  coarser.
- Tunables bounded by a **mechanism** (texel floors, the atlas limit, depth
  precision) are tied to that mechanism, not to the current geometry — and where
  a geometry change can break one, the limit is computed and logged (or clamped)
  rather than relied on.

The numbers in this document (in parentheses, "currently …") are worked examples
at today's values, for sizing budgets — not specification.

Reference templates: `templates/rooms/grid-canvases` (raster + coffer light +
mirror + bloom — the closest relative), `templates/rooms/hex-partitions`
(free-standing `Outward` rings), `templates/open-space` (monumental scale
concerns: far plane, bake budgets).

## Stage 1 — the room stage

### 1.1 Plan as ring data

Currently `ColSpace = 14`, `ColHeight = 40`, `ColWidth = 2`,
`BalkHeight = ColWidth · 3.4`, `ColsXHalf = 3`, `ColsZHalf = 6`. Derived:
`HalfX = ColsXHalf · ColSpace`, `HalfZ = ColsZHalf · ColSpace` (the wall planes,
currently 42 / 84), `BayWidth = ColSpace − ColWidth` (currently 12),
`SoffitY = ColHeight − BalkHeight` (currently 33.2) — the top of every wall and
column, where the balks take over (same role as `WallTopY` in the templates).

Column set: lattice node `(ix, iz)` carries a column iff `|ix| ≥ ColsXHalf − 1`
or `|iz| ≥ ColsZHalf − 1`.

| Piece                                                                           | Built as                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Perimeter wall + engaged columns (`\|ix\| = ColsXHalf` or `\|iz\| = ColsZHalf`) | **One `Inward` ring with pilaster notches**: the wall plane at `x = ±HalfX`, `z = ±HalfZ`, and every engaged column a `ColWidth/2 × ColWidth` protrusion into the room (corners: `ColWidth/2` square). Generated by a small local function walking the four sides (currently 32 pilasters + 4 corners). |
| Free columns (currently 28)                                                     | `Ring.rect(…, Facing.Outward, height = SoffitY)` at each remaining node.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Footprint                                                                       | `Footprint(outer +: columns)` → `floorBoundary` for walls, floor grime and `confine`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Balks                                                                           | `familyBeams` × 2 families (`dir` X and Z, `spacing = ColSpace`, `phase = 0`, `width = ColWidth`, `height = BalkHeight`, soffit `SoffitY`) → `2·ColsXHalf+1` + `2·ColsZHalf+1` balks (currently 7 + 13). `BeamAtlas` builds sides + soffit and drops outward faces.                                     |

Why the notched ring rather than overlapping column rings on the wall line:
`Boundary` is even-odd and classifies vertices per ring. Overlapping rings would
put no vertex at the real junction of pilaster side and wall, so the edge fade
would draw a seam there and `confine` would see a parity hole in the embedded
half. The notched ring gets inner junctions as `corner` and pilaster edges as
`arris` for free — and **each bay comes out as its own `Wall`**, which is
exactly the unit stage 2 paints on.

Why the balks are clipped against an **explicit rect** (outer faces of the
perimeter balks, `±(HalfX + ColWidth/2) × ±(HalfZ + ColWidth/2)`) rather than
`ceilingBoundary`: the perimeter balks are centered on the wall line (half
embedded), unlike the templates' `snapHalfExtent` convention, and a clip line
running along the notched ring's bay edges is collinear with them — degenerate
for `clipLine`. A plain rect is plain data, so building `Boundary(Ring(rect))`
inline is within the stage's contract. The same rect is what `atWall` in the
beam bake measures against.

Free columns stand under balk crossings with their faces flush with the balk
sides; pilaster fronts are flush with the perimeter balk's inner side. Both are
the template's "wall plane continues into the beam" situation.

### 1.3 Ambience bakes

The material is the templates' (tint × `roomNoise` × `grime`, faded at every
edge), but **what is baked, and how it is partitioned into textures, is this
sketch's own** — not one finished color texture per `Wall` as in the templates.

**Three layers, each at its own resolution, composed per fragment in the surface
shade** — never pre-composited into one big image, which would force every
surface to the finest layer's resolution:

| Layer    | Content                                   | Resolution                     |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| ambience | noise + grime **fields** only (see below) | `AmbienceTexScale` (~16 /m)    |
| detail   | floor slabs (1.6), paintings (2.5)        | own scale per kind (~48–64 /m) |
| grain    | one periodic tile shared by all paintings | `GrainTexScale` (~512 /m)      |

**Ambience partitions — continuous unwrapped strips.** `TextureBaker` rasterizes
a form by its UVs and evaluates the field at world position, so any multi-face
form with a non-overlapping UV layout bakes into one texture. Noise continuity
across partitions is then **free**: the field is world-space, every texel reads
the same function regardless of which texture it lands in.

| Partition   | Layout                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Count (currently) |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| perimeter   | the notched ring's walls **unwrapped in ring order** (bay, pilaster side, front, side, bay, …), cut greedily into strips at a face edge whenever the next face would push the strip past the texture limit | 2 (currently)     |
| free column | its 4 faces unwrapped around it into one strip                                                                                                                                                             | 28                |
| balks       | one `BeamAtlas` for all balks while it fits the limit; it takes any `Arr[Beam]`, so splitting per family is just calling it per subset                                                                     | 1 (currently)     |
| floor       | the bounding box, split into a grid of quads only when a side × scale would exceed the texture limit (computed, see below)                                                                                 | 1 (currently)     |

Two rules make partition breaks invisible:

- **Unwrap in geometric order.** Adjacent texels in a strip are adjacent on the
  surface, so bilinear filtering and mips across a face boundary inside a strip
  are _correct_, not bleed — the same argument as `BeamAtlas`' band order.
- **Break a partition only at a geometric edge** (a corner or arris), never
  mid-face. At every edge the shade reads the `g` channel (no normal-varied
  term), which holds only the continuous world term, so both sides of a break
  agree there. The floor is the one surface split mid-plane; it has a single
  normal, so only clamp-to-edge at half a texel differs — invisible on a field
  with ~40 m features.

**Shapes are a separate partition from textures.** A shape samples any sub-rect
of a partition texture via its UVs, so draw granularity is chosen for binding
convenience: each bay is its own shape (it carries its painting panel) sampling
its slice of the side strip; the rest of a side is one shape. Currently ~75
shapes instead of ~255.

**Per-partition resolution.** Since partitions are independent textures, a
partition can carry its own scale — e.g. columns or balks finer than walls if
they read soft. Every texture size is computed from the constants and checked
against the device's `maxTextureDimension2D`: the long perimeter strip
(currently ~180 m → 2880 px at 16 /m) and the floor are the ones that grow.
Splitting the floor (and a perimeter strip, at a corner) is the response, not
lowering the scale.

#### The arris fade decides realism — view-dependent, so not baked

The decisive detail of the ambient material is the **arris fade** — where the
normal-varied term rounds off at a protruding column edge. Edges in general may
be soft: a wrapping corner (column meeting wall, wall meeting floor) shows both
faces, and a broad blend between them is what stops it reading as a seam. **An
arris is different: usually only one of its faces is visible.** Then the fade
has nothing to blend toward — the face just changes tone along its silhouette, a
band that depicts a neighbor the eye cannot see. That is what looks wrong, and
no fixed radius cures it: any width that reads right seen across the corner
reads wrong seen from one side.

So the arris fade is **view-dependent**: its radius scales with how visible the
neighboring face is.

- Every face here meets its neighbors at 90°, so the neighbor across an edge has
  normal `±tangent` (sign from the edge's kind: an arris turns away, a corner
  wraps). Its visibility is `dot(normalize(eye − wp), neighborNormal)`,
  smoothstepped over a small band and multiplied into the arris radius.
- Seen head-on from one side, the neighbor is back-facing → radius 0 → the face
  keeps its own field right up to the silhouette, a hard edge as a real stone
  edge reads. Walking around the corner, the neighbor comes into view and the
  fade opens up — and may be as soft as a corner's, because now both faces are
  there to blend.
- Corners and the floor / top lines keep a fixed, generously soft radius.

**No screen-space pass, no neighbor sampling.** This is not SSAO-like. A
deferred pass has to rediscover the geometry from depth and normal buffers, so
its cost grows with the fade's pixel radius. Here the geometry is known exactly
— every face is a rectangle meeting its neighbors at 90° — so each fragment
computes everything locally, in object space, at constant cost however wide the
fade or close the edge:

```
s, w      = edgeInfo           // meters from the face's left edge, face width
t         = face tangent       // direction of increasing s
toEye     = normalize(eye − wp)
visL      = smoothstep(0, VisBand, dot(toEye, −t))   // neighbor across the left arris
visR      = smoothstep(0, VisBand, dot(toEye,  t))   // … across the right arris
rL, rR    = arris ? Fades.arris · vis : Fades.corner  // per side, kind from the plan
edge      = smoothstep(0, rL, s) · smoothstep(0, rR, w − s)
          · smoothstep(0, Fades.plane, wp.y) · smoothstep(0, Fades.top, SoffitY − wp.y)
ambience  = lerp(g, r, edge)
```

(`max(r, ε)` guards the zero radius.) At a convex 90° edge the hidden neighbor's
outward normal is `±t`, which is all the visibility term needs.

What it ignores: **occlusion by other objects**. It asks whether the neighbor
faces the eye, not whether it is actually on screen — a neighbor face turned
toward the viewer but hidden behind another column still opens the fade. Rare in
a lattice at eye height and accepted. A screen-space pass would be the tool only
for geometry this sketch does not have (curved or arbitrary meshes).

**Which edges need data — and why one `vec4` is enough.** The ring data is read
on the CPU at mesh build only; the shader sees per-face attributes. Only the
left/right edges vary per face; every other edge is deterministic in this
geometry:

| Edge                      | Kind                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Data                                         |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| wall / column left, right | arris or corner, per face, from `Edge.arrisAtA`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | `edgeInfo = (s, width, leftKind, rightKind)` |
| wall / column bottom      | always the floor junction at `y = 0` — a wrapping corner, both faces visible: soft `Fades.plane` + grime                                                                                                                                                                               | none (`wp.y`)                                |
| wall / column top         | **never an arris**: column and pilaster faces continue flush into balk sides (no edge at all — balks are `ColWidth` wide by construction); bays and pilaster sides meet the soffit overhead as an inside corner                                                                        | none (`SoffitY − wp.y`, one `Fades.top`)     |
| balk run                  | never faded — a run ends in a wall or a crossing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | —                                            |
| balk soffit ↔ side        | arris. The soffit carries **only the world term everywhere** (as in the templates), so it has no fade to depict a hidden side with. A side face's neighbor is the soffit, always visible from below → fixed `Fades.arris`; the soffit/side tint blend also ramps on the side face only | from atlas UV (`soffitCenterDist`)           |
| balk side ↔ top           | arris whose top face is **never** visible from eye height → always radius 0                                                                                                                                                                                                            | none                                         |

So walls and columns need exactly one `vec4` per vertex (plus their tangent);
balks need no view term at all and derive everything from their atlas UV; the
floor uses the lattice distance. A column face fades to the world term at
`SoffitY`, and the balk side above it starts there at its arris, also at the
world term — so the flush column-into-balk plane stays continuous.

One real shading change this exposes and keeps: a free column's vertical arris
becomes an **inside** corner at `SoffitY`, where the two crossing balks' sides
meet over the open coffer. Convex below, concave above — the fade switching
character there follows the geometry.

**Deliberately specific, not general.** This trades generality for constant
per-fragment cost, and that is the point: a general solution (screen-space,
plan-wide distance chains) pays for geometry this sketch does not have. It rests
on invariants the geometry constants cannot break by themselves, but a
structural change can — re-check these before one:

- every face is a rectangle meeting its neighbors at 90°;
- balks are exactly `ColWidth` wide and every column stands under a crossing (so
  column tops are never a ledge);
- the eye stays below `SoffitY` (balk tops and the top faces never seen; dev
  free-fly excepted).

That needs the edge factor in the **surface shade, evaluated analytically per
fragment** — a bake cannot depend on the eye. It also frees the fade from the
bake's resolution (16 /m is 6 cm a texel). The bake keeps only what is smooth —
the trick `open-space` uses for its ground tile, applied to every surface:

- `ambience` is **affine in its edge factor**. The bake stores it at both ends —
  **r = open face (edge 1), g = at an edge (edge 0)** — plus the two grime
  fields in **b, a** (creep, patch). The shade computes the edge factor and does
  `lerp(g, r, edge)`, which reproduces the templates' value exactly at any edge
  width. Keep `ambience` affine when tuning it.
- **The edge factor is exact and cheap**, because every face here is a
  rectangle: each wall/column face carries a per-vertex `edgeInfo` — meters from
  its left edge (interpolated), its width, and the left/right fade radius picked
  from the boundary's own classification (`Fades.arris` where `Edge.arrisAtA`
  says the vertex turns away, `Fades.corner` where it wraps) — so the distance
  to its vertical edges is `min(s, width − s)`, to the floor `wp.y`, to its top
  `SoffitY − wp.y`. No `min` chain over the plan. Balks get the same from
  `BeamAtlas.crossEdgeDist(uv)`, which is already an analytic function of the
  interpolated UV. The floor's distance to walls and column bases is the
  analytic lattice distance (nearest node → box distance if that node carries a
  column, plus the four wall planes).
- **Tint, grime and the edge lerp are applied in the shade.** Grime gets the
  same benefit: its band is evaluated from an exact distance, so it is crisp
  regardless of bake resolution.
- **`Fades` are absolute and free of any texel floor**: `corner`, `plane`, `top`
  soft by eye; `arris` is the radius at full neighbor visibility. The eye
  position is already a per-frame uniform need (the visibility term), so a
  far-distance widening (`max(radius, dist · k)`) is available if distant edges
  shimmer — less likely now that most fades are soft.
- **Partition breaks stay seamless**: at a break on an arris, either the
  neighbor is hidden (radius 0, the break is behind a silhouette) or both faces
  are visible and both fade to the shared `g` value.

This also removes the bake's dependency on `edgeSetDist` / `edgeFade` — the
bakes are pure fields of world position and normal — and with it the ~250-term
`min` chain risk (see below).

**Is ~16 /m enough?** Yes, now. The ambience bake carries only the smooth fields
(features tens of meters, grime fields ~1 m); every sharp feature — arris, grime
band, slabs, paint — is analytic or in a detail layer.

The light factor the detail layers need (to light paint and slab variation) is
`lerp(g, r, edge) × grime`, computed in the shade — it is no longer a baked
alpha.

No vertical tint gradient question to settle: there is a ceiling (the light), so
the templates' `WallTintLow → WallTintHigh` settling toward `SoffitY` applies,
over a proportionally larger `TopFadeDepth`.

**Not used: `edgeSetDist` / `edgeFade`.** They unroll a `min` chain over every
plan edge (~250 terms here) and are built for bakes. With the edge factor at
runtime they would be per fragment, so this sketch measures distances
analytically instead (face-local `edgeInfo`, `crossEdgeDist`, lattice distance).
The plan's `Boundary` is still the source of the vertex classification and of
`confine`. The beam bake's `atWall` term (distance to the clip rect) is a
four-plane distance, also analytic.

### 1.4 Light plane — irregular bloom

As in `grid-canvases`: a luminous plane above the balk grid at
`LightY = ColHeight + CofferDepth` (`CofferDepth` relative to `BalkHeight`),
with the overhang **derived** by the template's formula (`LightY`, `SoffitY`,
room depth, eye height) — currently ~66 m at a 5 m coffer. Baked at a low
`LightTexScale` (~4–8 /m, it is smooth), `Rgba16Float`.

The emitter shader is the exhibition's slot. Here: **a low-frequency world-meter
fbm blending `LightDimColor` → `LightBrightColor`**, so the field dips **below
the bloom threshold** in places — some coffers glow and halo, others read as
plain sky. The dim end is a blue-gray, not a darkened warm white: a dimmed warm
emitter reads brown. That is the "irregular bloom contribution". Constraints
carried over: the peaks stay under the mirror re-bloom cap
(`LightColor · reflMix` + floor base < 1.0), and structure is in world meters.

**Pure world noise, no per-cell pattern.** The openings are big enough for light
to mix across them, and the space wide enough for it to bounce until a
coffer-by-coffer pattern would blur away — so brightness is never keyed to the
cell grid. The noise's feature size is tuned later to sit roughly in the range
of the coffer frequency, by eye, as an absolute value like every other material
detail.

### 1.5 Floor reflection, bloom, camera

- `GaussianMirrorReflection` over `aboveGround = light + balks + walls`,
  `alphaScale = LightY`; blur re-tuned for the distances.
- `Bloom` on an `Rgba16Float` scene panel, tone knee as in the templates.
- `floorBnd.confine`, eye locked at 1.7 with the dev-only free-fly.
- The debug shade, `Transform`-instanced columns/balks/walls and the ground quad
  are deleted.

### 1.6 Floor tiles (later step of stage 1)

A slab pattern on the floor in addition to the monochrome tint:

- Slab grid in world meters, `SlabSize` absolute (start 1.6 m), anchored at the
  origin — which is a lattice node, so joints meet the central columns;
  elsewhere they line up only when `ColSpace` happens to be a multiple.
- **Pattern baked once as a small periodic panel** (one slab period, joints and
  edge bevel), sampled with a Repeat sampler at `xz / period` — mips give the
  joint lines antialiasing for free (the shader DSL has no `fwidth`).
- Per-slab tint variation at runtime from a hash of `floor(xz / slab)`.
- Composited multiplicatively with the ambience bake in the floor shade, before
  the reflection mix (reflection takes `reflStrength` out of whatever the floor
  shows).

## Stage 2 — pillar paintings, painted into the walls

### 2.1 Where

Every **bay** — a wall of the outer ring with `width ≈ BayWidth`, selected by
geometry (`wall.width > ColSpace / 2`), not by index or count. Currently 36 bays
(2 × 12 long, 2 × 6 short) of 12 m. Pilaster and column faces stay plain.

### 2.2 Palette — room-wide choices, per-pillar picks

**A new room on every reload, deliberately** — unseeded `rand()`, as in
`pillar`. No fixed seed, no re-roll key.

Split `Pillar.scala`'s `Palette`:

- **Room palette**, built once: the 12 choices — 6 hues (with the random hue
  offset) × a dark and a light variation — exactly `colorChoises`.
- **Per pillar**: 4 picks from those 12 (`colors`); its quad pairs pick from
  these 4.

So all 36 paintings share one color world, each with its own 4-color subset.

### 2.3 Dimensions, in bay meters

- Width: `BayWidth · randInRange(PillarWidthMin, PillarWidthMax)` with a narrow
  range (start 0.36–0.46, currently 4.3–5.5 m), centered — generous padding to
  the columns, slight variation.
- Bottom: `PillarFloorGap` (~0.4 m, a few grime widths — tied to the grime band,
  not the geometry), the same for all.
- Top: `bottom + randInRange(1/3, 0.95) · SoffitY` — strong variation, from a
  third of the wall to just under the balk.

### 2.4 Algorithm — unchanged from `paintings/pillar`

Height segmentation (`nextHeightSegment`, `MinHeight`/`MaxHeight` as fractions),
two jittered quads per segment, `HeightGap`, `VertexJitter` relative to width,
HSV variation per quad, sort by value. All of it is relative to width/height, so
it is scale-invariant and ports as a function of `(width, height, picks)`
returning quads in bay-local meters. Lives in a second sketch file
(`Pillars.scala`), CPU only.

### 2.5 Rendering — a second panel per bay

The template's recommendation for wall artwork: **its own panel at its own
resolution, sampled next to the ambience by a shade this sketch writes.**

- **Painting panel** per bay, covering only the painting's rect (+ jitter
  margin) at `PaintTexScale` (~48 /m → ≤ ~290 × 1550 px), cleared to
  transparent, quads drawn with alpha blending → premultiplied coverage. Needs
  the alpha half of the blend as `One / OneMinusSrcAlpha`; check
  `BlendState.Alpha`, else a local `BlendState`.
- **Bay shade**: the wall shade (1.3) plus the painting panel, sampled through
  the painting rect in bay-local meters — which the face's `edgeInfo` already
  carries (`s` from the left edge, `wp.y` up); outside the rect, coverage is 0.
  With `light = lerp(g, r, edge) × grime`:
  `color = tint · light · (1 − A) + paint.rgb · light` — the paint sits **in**
  the wall, lit by the same ambience and grime field as the plaster around it,
  and the arris fade at the bay's edges stays analytic.
- **Grain — one shared, high-resolution tile.** `Pillar`'s alpha grain
  (`(pos · 380).simplexNoise()`) is centimeter-scale here, far finer than
  `PaintTexScale`. So it gets its own texture: **one periodic noise tile**
  (grain features start ~1 cm; `GrainTileWorld` ~1–2 m at `GrainTexScale` ~512
  /m, single channel), baked once with `extendedFbmValue(tilingPeriod = …)` —
  integer domain period, see `open-space`'s `snapScale` — and sampled with a
  Repeat sampler in bay-local meters. Every painting reads the same grain; a
  random per-bay UV offset keeps neighboring bays from lining up. Better than
  computing simplex in the shade: one sample instead of a noise evaluation, and
  the **mip chain filters it at distance**, where runtime noise at this
  frequency would shimmer. Applied as a modulation of coverage `A` in the bay
  shade — not bit-identical to per-quad alpha, but in `Pillar` every quad at a
  pixel gets the same noise value, so it differs only where quads overlap.
- Bays are already in `aboveGround`, so the mirror reflects the paintings with
  no extra work.
